The end of

A young man in Teheran. Years of saving. One real bed… and one very windy night.

Tonight we read a story with a twist – and learn how English tells us what happened first.

Part 1

Reading

4 scenes. Hover the dotted words for meaning.
Purple = sentence patterns we will steal later.

Scene 1 · The dream
Tired of sleeping on the floor, a young man in Teheran saved up for years to buy a real bed.
For the first time in his life, he became the proud owner of a bed which had springs and a mattress.
🤔 Why "a real bed"? What had he been sleeping on before? How long did he save?

Structure spotting: "Tired of…," opens the sentence – who is tired? (the young man). Keep this pattern!

Scene 2 · On the roof
Because the weather was very hot, he carried the bed on to the roof of his house.
He slept very well for the first two nights, but on the third night, a storm blew up.
🤔 Is sleeping on the roof normal in a hot country? Why "but"? What do you expect next?

Traditional Iranian houses have flat roofs and a courtyard (院子) in the middle – remember that word!

Scene 3 · The storm
A gust of wind swept the bed off the roof and sent it crashing into the courtyard below.
The young man did not wake up until the bed had struck the ground.
Although the bed was smashed to pieces, the man was miraculously unhurt.
🤔 Which happened first – the bed hit the ground, or he woke up? How do you know?

"had struck" – past perfect: the earlier of two past actions. We'll build a timeline for this.

Scene 4 · Back to the floor
When he woke up, he was still on the mattress.
Glancing at the bits of wood and metal that lay around him, the man sadly picked up the mattress and carried it into his house.
After he had put it on the floor, he promptly went to sleep again.
🤔 Why is the last sentence funny? Where is he sleeping now? So… what was "the end of the dream"?

The story is a circle: floor → bed → floor. "Promptly went to sleep again" – he doesn't even care!

Grammar · Past perfect

Which came first?

Tap the dots in the order the events really happened.

The bed had struck the ground.
EARLIER → past perfect
He woke up.
LATER → past simple
He went to sleep again.
NOW the story ends
not … until … had done
He did not wake up until the bed had struck the ground.
= 直到床砸到地上他才醒。
Your turn: I didn't stop reading until I ____ (finish) the book.
After … had done, … did
After he had put it on the floor, he promptly went to sleep.
= 先放床垫，后睡觉。
Your turn: After we ____ (eat) dinner, we went for a walk.

Tap each rule card to see the answer frame. Elicit: finished / had eaten.

Grammar · Sentence surgery

Make it shorter, make it better

The text starts two sentences with -ed / -ing phrases instead of "Because he…". Let's do the same.

➜
Rule: the person doing the -ing / feeling the -ed must be the subject of the main sentence. ✗ Glancing at the wood, the mattress was picked up. (the mattress can't glance!)

Students say the answer aloud first, then reveal.

Wrap-up

So… was it really the end of the dream?

💬 Talk (2 min)

  • He "sadly picked up the mattress" but "promptly went to sleep again". Is he sad or happy? Why?
  • Change ONE thing to change the ending. (e.g. If he had tied the bed to the roof…)
  • Have you ever saved up for something? What happened to it?

📝 Homework

  • Retell the story in 60–80 words. Use: not…until…had, After…had, and ONE sentence starting with -ed/-ing.
  • Sentence Builder Level 5: Combine + Fix.
  • Write 3 true sentences: For the first time in my life, …

Exit ticket: each student says one sentence beginning with "Tired of…" before leaving.
