Reading between the lines

Why is it funny?

The sister says three things. None of them means what it says. Click each line to go one layer deeper.

Three layers

LAYER 1 · WHAT SHE SAYS"This is the prettiest carpet I have ever seen."
What she means: "Your floor is a disaster." Saying the opposite of what you mean = irony. The clue is the exaggeration: prettiest … ever.
LAYER 2 · THE ADVICE"You don't need bookcases at all."
She has just carried a heavy bookcase up the stairs for him. Now she says he doesn't need it. The "advice" is a tease, not a solution.
LAYER 3 · THE PUNCHLINE"…sit here in your spare time and read the carpet!"
A pun: you read books, you sit on carpets – here you can do both. The joke works because the carpet really is made of books. Note the timing: she gazed at it for some time, then added – the pause makes the punchline land.

Now you read the carpet

Twelve books on the floor, one word each. Flip a book, say its sentence, move to the next – and you have retold the story.

How to play

Round 1: in order, one book each. Round 2: shuffle – whoever gets the book must say its sentence and the one before it.
